Research

The intergalactic and circumgalactic medium

The gas between and around galaxies records how they acquire fuel and lose it again. The question here is how the Mg II absorbers seen in the spectra of background quasars connect to the galaxies near the sightline.

Analysing mock quasar absorption spectra from IllustrisTNG and SIMBA, this asks whether those absorbers come from outflowing material expelled by star-forming galaxies, or from large rotating disks accreting material onto them.

IllustrisTNG, SIMBA
Method
Mock quasar spectra

O VI in the intragroup medium

Compares O VI absorption in synthetic spectra from SIMBA and TNG50 galaxy groups against COS-IGrM observations, using matched group samples and observation-like synthetic spectroscopy to study absorber distributions, kinematics, covering fractions and galaxy associations.

Kinematics of the outer circumgalactic medium

Investigating co-rotation and counter-rotation of outer CGM gas around L-star galaxies, using sightline-based synthetic spectroscopy in TNG50-1.

Spectral classification of AGN and supernovae

Telling the spectral signatures of active galactic nuclei apart from those of supernovae matters for characterising transient events. This work uses Bayesian logic (BLOG) to build automated classification from emission-line detections, widths and ratios.

No absorption trace: this is an emission-line study, so there is nothing for a background source to be absorbed against.
